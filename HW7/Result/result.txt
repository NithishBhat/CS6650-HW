Phase 1: Sync Tests
Normal (5 users, 30s): 40 requests, 0 failures, avg 3,046ms, P95 3,100ms, 1.35 RPS. System handled normal load fine.
Flash Sale (20 users, 60s): 285 requests, 0 failures, avg 3,678ms, P95 4,900ms, P99 5,100ms, 4.90 RPS. No failures but latency spiked 70%. Throughput capped at ~5 RPS.
Phase 2: Bottleneck Math

Max throughput: 15 slots ÷ 3s = 5 orders/sec
Flash sale demand: ~55 orders/sec
Orders lost per second: ~50

Phase 3: Async Test
Flash Sale (20 users, 60s): 3,289 requests, 0 failures, avg 57ms, P95 88ms, 54.97 RPS.
Speedup: 3,678ms / 57ms = 64.5x faster. Accepted 11.5x more orders than sync.
Phase 4: Queue Buildup (1 worker)

Part III: Lambda
Cold Starts

Cold start init: ~73ms avg (72-75ms range)
Overhead on 3s processing: 2.4% — negligible
Warm starts: ~3001-3004ms, no overhead
4 concurrent Lambda instances spun up automatically

Cost

ECS: $17/month (always running)
Lambda: $0/month (free up to 267K orders/month)

Trade-offs

Lambda: zero ops overhead, auto-scaling, but loses messages after 2 SNS retries
ECS + SQS: more ops burden, but SQS retains messages for 4 days

Recommendation
Lambda is the right choice for a startup under 267K orders/month. The cost and operational savings outweigh the retry limitations, which can be mitigated with a dead-letter queue.
